Research

My research combines geographic information science, environmental engineering and urban design to examine urban heat, greenery and climate resilience. I integrate field observations, remote sensing, urban morphology and population data through GeoAI and microclimate modelling to map thermal exposure and evaluate cooling strategies. A central focus is who benefits from cooling, where and when, translating evidence into practical guidance for equitable urban greening and climate adaptation.

GeoAI & Spatial Environmental Modelling

I integrate remote sensing, weather data, field observations and three-dimensional urban morphology to model environmental conditions at city, neighbourhood and pedestrian scales. My work uses neural networks, ensemble learning and downscaling to develop interpretable evidence for urban decision-making.

Related work: Citywide hourly mapping of thermal stress · Large-scale outdoor thermal comfort assessment

Urban Heat & Exposure Equity

I study how heat exposure varies across urban environments and population groups, and how thermal conditions influence perception and travel behaviour. This research links environmental risk with vulnerability to identify priorities for equitable cooling interventions.

Related work: Urban tree cooling and exposure inequality · Demographic inequities in diurnal shelter shade

Nature-Based Solutions & Climate Adaptation

I evaluate the cooling and energy-saving potential of urban trees, green spaces, green roofs and cool roofs under current and future climates. My wider interests include wetlands, biodiversity, sustainable resource management and climate-resilient urban development.

Related work: Roof strategies under current and future climates · Cooling and energy-saving potential in 43 megacities
